# Conversation Notes - ₱1.5M Execution Capital and IFX Discovery

## Initial Funding Context

A prospective backer may provide ₱1.5M.

She wants to understand:

- how she may recover her money when larger funding arrives
- whether interim cash flow can be generated while waiting
- whether x-change can become commercially real under practical conditions

## Strategic Reframing

The capital should be framed as Execution Capital, not ordinary growth capital.

The purpose is to reduce execution risk and produce tangible assets.

The backer may be positioned as a Founding Execution Partner.

## Promised Delivery

The intended delivery promise includes:

- x-change package fully productized
- x-Payout fully productized
- commercial branding
- patent/IP work advanced
- product ready for commercialization to banks, SMEs, and high-net-worth individuals
- 3-5 paying companies using the platform
- investor roadshows considered, but deprioritized because another party may already be handling investor outreach

## Refined Pillars

The engagement should focus on two main pillars:

1. Build Commercial Assets
2. Validate the Business

Growth pipeline activity is secondary unless it directly supports funding recovery or commercial validation.

## IFX Discovery

A possible first reference customer is IFX, a money changer outfit owned by the founder's college friend.

IFX has branches in high-end malls.

Observed pain:

Sometimes late in the day, branches display signs such as "No more PHP."

This means the branch cannot buy foreign currency from customers because it lacks peso cash.

In a mall, customers can easily walk to a nearby competitor.

In SM malls, there may be multiple money changers, including customer-service counters that also perform foreign exchange.

The business problem is therefore not abstract fintech modernization.

The business problem is lost revenue when a customer is ready to transact but the branch lacks cash.

## Cash Continuity Insight

x-change / x-Payout could allow the money changer to complete the transaction even without handing over physical pesos.

Instead of receiving cash, the customer may receive digital settlement through GCash, Maya, bank transfer, or another settlement option.

Pay Code may exist behind the scenes as the settlement instruction, but the customer should not initially be asked whether they want a "Pay Code" because customers do not yet know what that means.

The customer-facing offer should likely be:

"Would you like us to send the proceeds to GCash or Maya instead?"

or even:

"Do you have a phone that can scan a QR code or browse the internet? We can still complete the exchange."

## Product Discovery

The important customer capability may not be identity at first.

It may be whether the customer has a smartphone that can scan a QR code or browse the internet.

A possible flow:

1. Branch runs out of PHP cash.
2. Customer still wants to sell foreign currency.
3. Cashier offers digital settlement.
4. Customer scans QR code or opens a web page.
5. Customer chooses GCash, Maya, bank account, or another option.
6. x-change issues or redeems the settlement instruction behind the scenes.
7. IFX preserves the transaction and earns the spread.

## Value Proposition

Potential headline:

"Never lose a foreign exchange transaction simply because the branch ran out of pesos."

Alternative framing:

"Cash Continuity."

This is not merely digital transformation.

It is revenue protection and transaction continuity.

## Adoption Objection

If the product works as described, many money changers and micro-lenders may want it.

The strongest expected objection is not the product.

The strongest expected objection is trust in the settlement rail.

Some customers or merchants may not trust smaller financial partners such as NetBank or Paynamics.

This validates the architectural need for x-change to support multiple settlement rails.

The proper response to a rail-trust objection is:

"Which bank or settlement partner do you trust?"

The merchant should buy x-change for business continuity while choosing settlement rails that fit its own compliance and trust requirements.

## IFX Discovery Conversation

The first conversation with IFX should begin with discovery, not a pitch.

A possible opening question:

"Pare, may mga araw ba na kailangan ninyong tumanggi sa customer dahil naubusan na kayo ng pesos?"

or:

"Have you ever lost a customer because your branch had already run out of pesos?"

The goal is to let IFX explain:

- whether peso supply shortages happen
- how often they happen
- why they happen
- what customers do
- whether customers come back
- how much revenue may be lost
- whether digital settlement would help
